# Nexus Forge Gaming Moderation Policy

**Draft pending attorney review. This document is not final.**

Version 1.0 (draft)
Effective date: the date the operator publishes this document
Operator: TRH Development, a sole proprietorship owned and operated by Hank

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

**1.1** This Moderation Policy explains how Nexus Forge Gaming ("Nexus Forge Gaming," "we," "us," or "our") receives, reviews and decides reports and other concerns about conduct on the Services, and what actions we take. It applies to Nexus Forge Gaming staff, to Forge owners and moderators acting within their permissions, and to automated tools such as AutoMod.

**1.2** This Policy puts into practice the rules in the Acceptable Use Policy and the Community Rules. Capitalized terms that this Policy does not define have the meanings given in the Terms of Service.

**1.3** We do not review every message. We act when we receive a report, when we detect a serious risk, or when the law requires us to act.

---

## 2. Who reviews reports and makes decisions

**2.1 Nexus Forge Gaming staff.** Our staff review reports about direct messages, escalated channel reports, safety concerns and appeals. They decide platform-level actions such as content removal, restrictions, suspensions and permanent bans. Staff access is limited to what their role requires, and their actions are recorded.

**2.2 Forge owners and moderators.** The owner of a Forge, and members who hold the moderate-chat permission in that Forge, review reports about channel messages in their Forge. They can see only reports from channels they are allowed to open. They can act only within their Forge and only under their permissions. They cannot see reports about direct messages.

**2.3 Automated tools.** Forge owners can set AutoMod rules that check messages for blocked words, links, invites and mention spam. Depending on the rule, AutoMod can block a message, alert moderators or time a member out. AutoMod acts only on the rules the Forge owner has set. Security signals, such as rate limits and alerts about sign-ins from new devices, protect accounts. They do not by themselves decide that someone has broken these rules.

**2.4 People decide accounts.** Decisions about accounts are made by people. Platform-level actions are taken by Nexus Forge Gaming staff. Forge-level actions are taken by Forge owners and moderators.

**2.5 Conflicts.** No one reviews a report about themselves, about someone they are in a personal dispute with, or about content they sent. A staff member who reviews an appeal must not be the person who made the original decision (see the Appeals Policy).

---

## 3. How reports reach us

**3.1 In-app reports.** Choose Report on a message or on a profile. Pick the closest reason: Spam, Harassment, Hate, Sexual, Violence, Self-harm, Scam or Other. You can add a short description. Reports about channel messages go to the moderators of that Forge, and Nexus Forge Gaming staff can also review them. Reports about direct messages go to Nexus Forge Gaming staff.

**3.2 Copies of reported content.** When you report a message, we keep a copy of the message, and any attachments, as they were when reported. This lets the report be reviewed even if the message is later edited or deleted.

**3.3 Support and problem reports.** Support tickets, such as those in the "Report a player" category, and submissions through the Report a problem form on the Support page at https://nfgcord.com/support reach Nexus Forge Gaming staff. The Report a problem form does not require an account, so you can use it if you cannot sign in. Safety concerns can be submitted under the "Safety or abuse" option.

**3.4 Other sources.** We also receive matters from law enforcement, from legal process and from copyright notices, which the Intellectual Property Policy governs.

**3.5 Automated signals.** Abuse-prevention and security signals can prompt a staff review. A signal on its own does not lead to an action against an account.

---

## 4. Triage and time targets

**4.1 Priorities.** When we triage a report, we place it in one of four groups:

- **Urgent safety.** Sexual content involving minors, credible threats of violence, imminent self-harm, active doxxing with a threat, and violent emergencies in progress.
- **Serious.** Doxxing, hate combined with threats, non-consensual intimate images, scams at scale, malware, fraud, ban evasion and serious security attacks.
- **Standard.** Harassment, spam, impersonation, economy abuse, promotion of gambling or lotteries, and recording or sharing private calls.
- **Low.** Other reports, duplicates and general questions.

**4.2 Targets.** We aim to review urgent safety reports as soon as possible, and to review every other report as promptly as we can. We do not promise a fixed response time for each report.

**4.3 Grouping.** Duplicate reports about the same content are grouped. Several reports about the same content help to show that it needs review, but they do not by themselves prove that a rule was broken.

**4.4 Reporter history.** We also look at reports that appear to be used to harass someone or to make false complaints. Those reports can lead to action under Section 2.17 of the Acceptable Use Policy.

## 5. The review process

**5.1 Steps.** Each review follows these steps:

1. Record the report and its reason.
2. Triage it by priority.
3. Gather the evidence described in Section 6.
4. Assess the conduct against the Acceptable Use Policy and the Community Rules, taking the context into account.
5. Decide: no action, a warning, removal, restriction, suspension, permanent ban, or referral.
6. Tell the affected person what we decided (Section 9).
7. Tell the reporter the outcome in general terms. We may say whether the report led to action, but we do not share details of any action taken against someone else.
8. Record the decision (Section 10).
9. Handle any appeal under the Appeals Policy.

**5.2 Standard.** We decide on the evidence we have. Where the evidence is unclear and the potential harm is low, we generally take no action or give a warning. Where the potential harm is serious, we act quickly to reduce the risk, and then complete the review.

---

## 6. Evidence and private messages

**6.1 What we consider.** We consider:

- the reported content, as it was when reported;
- the surrounding conversation in the same channel or thread, to understand context;
- the reporter's reason and details;
- the account's record of earlier warnings, restrictions and suspensions;
- information needed to identify ban evasion or a compromised account;
- relevant Forge audit log entries; and
- anything the person affected tells us.

**6.2 Forge channels.** Messages in a Forge channel can be seen by the members who can access that channel. Moderators and staff may review them as needed for a report.

**6.3 Private messages.** Direct messages and group direct messages are private. We do not read them as part of routine monitoring. We access a private message only:

- (a) when a user has reported it, and then only the reported messages and the limited context needed to understand them;
- (b) when a safety emergency requires it to prevent serious harm, and only to the extent necessary; or
- (c) when the law requires it, such as valid legal process or a legal obligation to make a report, for example to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children.

**6.4 Limits.** In each case we look at the fewest messages that answer the question. We do not browse anyone's conversations.

**6.5 Logging.** Each access to a private message is recorded with its reason, the reviewer and the date.

**6.6 No call recordings.** The Services do not record voice or video calls, so there are no call recordings to review.

**6.7 Age documents.** We use age-verification documents only to check age. We do not use them to review conduct.

## 7. Standards for actions

**7.1 Starting points.** The table below shows where we usually start. Each case is judged on its facts, and we may act more or less severely than the starting point.

| Conduct | Usual first action | Escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spam or link flooding | Removal; warning | Repeated after a warning: temporary restriction, then suspension |
| Minor or single incident of harassment | Warning; removal | Repeated, targeted or sustained: temporary restriction, then suspension |
| Serious harassment | Removal; suspension | Ongoing or coordinated: permanent ban |
| Hate | Removal; temporary restriction | Repeated or severe: suspension or permanent ban |
| Threats of violence | Immediate removal; suspension; urgent review | Permanent ban; referral to law enforcement where required |
| Doxxing | Removal; suspension | With a threat or intent to endanger: permanent ban |
| Adult sexual content | Removal; temporary restriction | Repeated or sustained: suspension or permanent ban |
| Sexual content involving minors | Immediate removal; permanent ban | Reporting as set out in Section 8.2 |
| Non-consensual intimate images | Immediate removal; permanent ban | Referral to law enforcement where required |
| Scams, phishing or account trading | Removal; suspension | Repeated or at scale: permanent ban; Coins or items removed or frozen |
| Malware or exploits | Immediate removal; suspension | Permanent ban |
| Ban evasion | Restriction of the new account | Permanent ban |
| Economy cheating | Removal of gained Coins or items; restriction | Repeated or organized: suspension or permanent ban |
| Impersonation | Removal; warning | Deceptive or harmful: suspension |
| Gambling or lottery promotion, or selling Coins for money | Removal; restriction | Repeated: suspension |
| Recording or sharing private calls | Removal; warning | Repeated or intimidating: suspension |
| Misuse of moderator powers | Removal of moderator powers in the Forge; restriction | Serious abuse: suspension on the platform |

**7.2 Factors.** We consider:

- the severity and the harm caused;
- intent;
- whether the conduct was isolated or repeated;
- whether the Content was public, private or both;
- whether the person affected is a minor or is otherwise vulnerable;
- the person's history on the Services;
- whether the person accepted responsibility and stopped;
- whether the account may have been compromised; and
- whether the conduct is also a crime.

**7.3 Forge and platform actions are separate.** A Forge ban does not require a platform action, and a platform action does not require a Forge action.

## 8. Emergencies and urgent safety referrals

**8.1 Immediate action.** Where there is a risk of imminent serious harm, we may act before a review is complete. We may remove Content, restrict an account, disable a feature or end a live session. We record why we acted and review the action as soon as practicable.

**8.2 Sexual content involving minors.** We remove the content immediately and stop access by the account involved. We report apparent child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) through its CyberTipline, and to law enforcement, as required by 18 U.S.C. § 2258A. We preserve information only as the law requires, and keep no more copies than are necessary to report and to comply with the law. We follow any legal restriction on telling an account holder about a report.

**8.3 Threats and self-harm.** These reports are reviewed urgently. Where the information allows and the law permits, we contact local emergency services. We show crisis resources, including, in the United States, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

**8.4 Law enforcement and legal process.** We respond to valid legal process as described in the Privacy Policy and applicable law. We disclose information in an emergency when the law permits and the emergency requires it. We check the legal basis for each request, disclose only what is requested and permitted, and record each request.

**8.5 Minors.** The Services are for people aged 18 and over. We close accounts that belong to people under 18. Where we identify a minor's use of the Services, we apply the protections of the Texas SCOPE Act (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 509) to the extent that Act applies to the Services.

## 9. Notices to users

**9.1 The affected person.** We tell you what action we took, the rule that applies, whether the action is temporary, which features it affects (including Coins), and how to appeal. We do not give more detail than needed for safety or as the law allows.

**9.2 Suspensions.** We tell you the reason for the suspension and whether it ends at a set time or stays until we lift it. The notice appears in the app, and the details are sent to the email address on the account.

**9.3 Reporters.** We do not tell the person you reported who reported them. We may tell you whether action was taken on your report, without giving details of any action against someone else.

**9.4 Exceptions.** Where the law forbids notice, for example in some legal requests, or where a safety concern requires confidentiality, we may withhold notice.

**9.5 Forge actions.** Forge owners and moderators should tell members the reason for a Forge action where practical.

---

## 10. Records and the audit trail

**10.1 Decision records.** For each decision we keep a record that includes:

- the report or other source reference;
- the date and time;
- the reviewer's role and internal identifier;
- the section of the Acceptable Use Policy or Community Rules relied on;
- the evidence considered, including the copy of any reported content;
- the decision and the reasons for it;
- the actions taken and the notices sent; and
- any appeal and its outcome.

**10.2 Forge audit log.** Forge actions are also recorded in that Forge's audit log, which the Forge owner and Nexus Forge Gaming administrators can see.

**10.3 Retention and access.** We keep moderation records for as long as needed to keep the Services safe, to handle appeals and to meet legal obligations. Access is limited to people who need it for those purposes, and is handled under the Privacy Policy.

**10.4 Purpose.** The audit trail lets us check consistency, correct errors and show how a decision was reached. When a decision is reversed, the record is kept and marked as reversed.

---

## 11. Quality and consistency

**11.1** We review a sample of decisions regularly to check consistency and correct mistakes.

**11.2** Staff and moderators must follow this Policy. Moderators who misuse their powers can lose them, as set out in the Community Rules.

---

## 12. Transparency

**12.1** We publish periodic summaries of enforcement activity. The summaries show the number of reports received, the actions taken by category, and the appeals and their outcomes. The summaries contain no personal information.

**12.2** Where the law allows, reports we make to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children and legal requests we receive are counted in those summaries.

## 13. Texas law

**13.1 Minors.** Section 8.5 describes the Texas SCOPE Act protections that apply to the Services.

**13.2 Privacy rights.** The Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541) gives Texas consumers rights over their personal data, including a right to appeal a refusal of a consumer request. The Appeals Policy and the Privacy Policy explain how to use those rights.

**13.3 Criminal law.** Some conduct in the Acceptable Use Policy is also a crime under Texas law. Those laws apply independently, and our decisions under this Policy are separate from any criminal process.

**13.4 Complaints.** Complaints to the Texas Attorney General are described in the Appeals Policy.

---

## 14. Appeals

Appeals of decisions by Nexus Forge Gaming staff are handled under the Appeals Policy. Decisions by a Forge's owners and moderators are handled first within that Forge.
